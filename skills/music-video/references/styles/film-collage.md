# Film collage

Archival and found footage, photos and the person's own material, cut and layered like a memory, with real film
texture and type set in the gaps.

## Medium

- Footage: public-domain archives (Prelinger via Internet Archive, NASA, national film archives), the person's
  own videos and photos, stock only where it truly fits. Same period or deliberately mixed periods, decided in
  the spec.
- Film: 16 mm look: grain 6–9%, gate weave ≤ 1 px, halation on highlights (red-orange, radius 6–12 px, 20%),
  slightly lifted blacks (5–8%), rounded frame corners optional, occasional frame-edge flash on cuts (2 frames,
  not on every cut).
- Photos: printed look (white border, slight curl shadow) or full-bleed with the same grain.
- Colour: one grade for all sources; match first, then the look.

## Type

A typewriter-era or grotesk face, white or the film's highlight colour, set in clear areas of the frame or on
black frames between clips, like intertitles. Hero lines as full-frame intertitles held one bar.

## Motion personality

Cuts on phrases in verses, on the bar in choruses. Footage speed ramps only on hits. Occasional multiple
exposure (two clips at 50% `screen`) on the bridge.

## Sections

- Verse: one clip per line, the line on an intertitle or in a clear area.
- Chorus: a faster montage around one recurring image (the home image) that returns at each chorus start.
- Bridge: a single long clip, slowed, double-exposed with the person.
- Outro: back to the opening clip, ending on a held frame.

## Build notes

Search archives with the shot list's visual description (light, motion, framing). Prefer intra-frame or
high-bitrate sources; conform all clips to the film's fps before editing. Ledger every clip; check rights per
item.

## How it goes wrong

Random pretty stock (no rule), modern 4K stock under fake grain (reads fake), grain that does not move, type over
busy footage without a plan.
