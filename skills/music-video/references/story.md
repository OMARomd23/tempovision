# Treatments: the film behind the song

A treatment is the idea of the film in words, before any picture. Three treatments that are three different
films let the person choose a direction, not a colour. Write them after the song map, because the structure
of the song is the structure of the film.

## Song structure is film structure

- **Sections are chapters.** Intro sets the world. Verses move the story or deepen the world. The pre-chorus
  builds pressure. The chorus is the home stage: it returns to the same place each time and escalates
  (bigger, closer, more of it, or the same image changed by what the verse revealed). The bridge breaks the
  pattern: a new place, a new scale, or stillness. The outro rhymes with the intro.
- **Energy is pace.** Low energy: long shots (2–4 bars), slow camera (a push of 2–6% over a phrase), few
  elements. High energy: a change every bar or every beat on the hit list, more contrast, more of the frame lit.
- **Phrases are shots.** A lyric line or a 2/4-bar musical phrase is the natural length of a shot. A cut in the
  middle of a sung word is a mistake unless it is the point.
- **Gaps carry the film.** An instrumental stretch has no words to lean on: give it the strongest pure image of
  that chapter, or the transition into the next world.

## What makes a music video treatment good

- **One governing image or rule** that the whole film obeys. "A paper city that folds itself up line by line
  until only the singer's room is left." "Every lyric is written by light falling through a window, and the
  window moves through one day." A rule creates variation without randomness.
- **The words drive the pictures.** Each hero line has its own visual idea that shows what it means. Concrete
  nouns in the lyrics are gifts: use them.
- **A turn.** Something changes between the first and the last chorus, so the last one means more.
- **An ending that rhymes with the opening**: the same frame changed, the same object transformed, a camera
  move reversed.
- **Fit to means.** A treatment you cannot build to a high standard with the brief's tools and budget is a
  bad treatment. Prefer a simple idea executed perfectly.

## Three different films

Make the three differ in kind, for example:

1. **Literal/narrative**: the lyrics' situation, staged (who, where, what happens).
2. **Metaphor**: one image system that stands for the song's feeling (weather, a building, a machine, a
   garden), developed through the song.
3. **Type-world**: the words themselves are the protagonists; the film is about how the lyrics live in a
   designed space.

Or two narratives in different media. What must differ: the governing rule, the medium, and what the chorus
does.

## Treatment format (each one, ~150–250 words)

```text
TITLE        a working title for the concept
LOGLINE      one sentence: the film, not the song
WORLD        place, medium and texture; the palette in 3 words; light
RULE         the governing image or rule
CHAPTERS     intro / verse / pre / chorus (and how it escalates on each return) / bridge / outro, one line each
HERO LINES   the 2–5 lines and the idea for each
TURN         what changes by the last chorus
ENDING       the last frame and what it rhymes with
WHY          why this song, in one or two sentences grounded in the lyric or the song map
MEANS        what it is built from (code-drawn / real / generated, share of each), and the risk
```

Recommend one, with a reason grounded in the brief and the song map. The person picks (or you pick in `full`
autonomy, recorded with `tempovision project decide`). Then `TREATMENT.md` holds only the chosen one, expanded:
add each chapter's frame range and the hit-list moments it uses.

## From treatment to shot list

`SHOTLIST.md` has one row per shot, in song order, with frame ranges from `SONGMAP.md` (frames = seconds ×
fps, rounded). Columns: `#`, `frames`, `section`, `lyric` (exact words or "—"), `picture` (what the viewer
sees, concretely), `hero move` (the one move), `type` (lyric treatment for this shot), `hit` (which hit it lands
on), `hand-off` (how it leads into the next shot), `assets`.

Rules for the list:

- Something new every 2–4 s; in high-energy sections a new layout every bar.
- No treatment held for more than ~6 s without a change.
- Each hero line has its own row with its own idea.
- Every cut has a reason (a hit, a phrase boundary, a lyric turn) written in the row.
- Chorus rows reference the first chorus ("C1 home stage, +scale") so the return is built as escalation.

## Characters, if any

Characters act, they do not pose: each shot gives them a verb. Design them once on a reference sheet (front,
three-quarter, profile, two expressions, palette) before any shot, and generate or draw every appearance from
that sheet (`assets.md` "Consistency"). A character drawn in code (SVG rig, cut paper) is consistent for free;
a generated one costs a reference sheet and careful prompting per shot.
